# MedPAi — Nigeria Care-Continuity North Star and Adversarial Product Strategy

**Research date:** 10 October 2026. **Decision state:** An ambitious, evidence-led *hypothesis*, not an authorized clinical triage product, existing government integration, licensed emergency service, or approved business. Do not conflate alignment with endorsement. MedPAi must never be designed as a patient-data monopoly.

## Founder's expanded vision and the product thesis

**Founder mandate:** Target a landmark in Nigeria, not just polished software; confront triage problems AND breakages in information across patient journeys, not medication history alone; research past, present and Nigeria's foreseeable reforms; invent a defensible, auditable and adaptable care infrastructure component that future clinicians and institutions **choose** to rely upon because it reliably closes loops. Ask hard and apparently impossible questions, measure reality, and protect patients and resources. Zero-cash-first during exploration; not pretend regulated healthcare operates free.

**Proposed North Star:** **MedPAi is an accountable care-transition memory and verification layer.** For ONE permitted journey, it captures what a trained provider knows or does not know at first contact; explicitly separates reported medication use and verified evidence; packages a minimum-necessary handoff with its source/uncertainties; tracks transmission and actual receiver acknowledgment; and tracks outcome/feedback across sites. It should allow systems to exchange information using evolving national standards, and continue safely when devices, networks, people or referral destinations fail.

**The hard business question:** Can MedPAi turn a care pathway that currently loses context, referrals and accountability into a documented *closed loop*, cheaper and safer than simply training staff or improving a paper form? This must be proven in Nigeria, not borrowed from Western studies.

**Do not build now:** Autonomous triage classifier, emergency dispatch, tele-doctor marketplace, national patient registry, full EMR, national drug registry, drug interaction clearance, herb dosing, pharmaceutical supply chain, mandatory single provider, or another central patient database.

## Primary source picture: what exists, what is in progress, and what is NOT confirmed

### Pre-existing failures and gaps — Nigeria, WHO/AHOP 2025 (updated Oct 2026)
- WHO/AHOP Nigerian Health Systems Profile **service delivery**, 7 Aug 2025 (updated Oct 2 2026), reports **suboptimal referral systems**, patients bypassing lower levels, insufficient emergency/pre-hospital capacity, limited PHC essential services and uneven specialist resources. It separately notes modern/traditional medicine coexistence with risk. https://ahop.afro.who.int/download/service-delivery-nigeria-health-systems-and-services-profile/
- WHO/AHOP **health information systems** chapter reports health information policies not routinely translating into real-time decision use, weak ICT, staffing, integration and data-use culture. https://ahop.afro.who.int/download/health-information-and-information-systems-nigeria-health-systems-and-services-profile/
- WHO/AHOP **medical products** highlights supply/quality problems, local manufacturing constraints and incomplete traditional-medicine standardization. This is why we should preserve NAFDAC source status and unknowns, not declare drug authenticity or safety. https://ahop.afro.who.int/download/medical-products-and-health-technologies-nigeria-health-system-and-services-profile/
- WHO Africa 11 Apr 2026 reports primary-care power outages; estimates **40–50%** of Nigeria's PHCs have unreliable electricity; documents Rumuigbo PHC in Rivers State where a midwife used a torch during labour. A credible physical constraint for power- and bandwidth-aware design, not an acceptable excuse for weak cybersecurity. https://afro.who.int/countries/nigeria/news/powering-care-protecting-lives-how-two-nigerian-communities-see-health-differently

### Roadmap interpretation — hypotheses, not current deployments
- 2026–2028: NDHI implementations and PHC strengthening will likely increase the value of interop-ready tools **if** funding, infrastructure, access, governance and adoption succeed. Don't rely on guaranteed timing or future government procurement.
- As medicines, referrals, results and EHRs become increasingly digitized, data interchange without **receipt, acknowledgment, provenance, role and outcome** still leaves care discontinuity. MedPAi might contribute that process layer; first demonstrate with two institutions and a paper baseline.
- Real opportunity may be a **portable, patient-permissioned care episode packet + closed-loop acknowledgment and status machine**. The value must be grounded in clinical operations, not solely data extraction or AI.

## One end-to-end workflow to PROVE (not all Nigeria)

**Candidate pilot:** An approved, non-emergency adult **hypertension outpatient referral** from one frontline facility/PHC or licensed outpatient clinic to one specific receiving secondary-care/outpatient team, with qualified professional oversight. Why: medication history is consequential; national hypertension policy/PHC reforms matter; can measure referrals, clinician comprehension and follow-up without simulating emergency triage. But referral facilities/buyer access harder than one pharmacy; validate against initial pharmacy medication-reconciliation wedge rather than assuming acceptance.

**Step 1 — first-contact capture:** A trained worker records *reported* presenting complaint, the reason for referral as authored by an authorized professional, onset/timing, known vital-sign observations with measurement origin, medication assertions (prescription ≠ dispense ≠ actual use), allergies/history only if lawfully available and verified status, traditional/OTC products as raw strings. No automated red/green severity.
**Step 2 — human triage/disposition:** Only clinical staff trained and authorized by an institution apply an approved care protocol. Urgent deterioration requires established in-person/emergency pathway **without waiting for MedPAi, network, or clinician login**. Product records clinician decision, its time, certainty and any missing information; no fabricated escalation.
**Step 3 — minimum-necessary handoff:** Create a clinician-reviewed episode bundle with source/time, reasons, performed actions, medication review, observations, current condition, outstanding unknowns, destination, responsible sender, consent/legal basis, time-limited access and simple readable rendering. Render paper-safe for downtime without public QR plaintext of health details.
**Step 4 — receiving-team ACK:** Receiver identity/organization/capacity verified; acknowledges *receipt*, not necessarily acceptance. Separate events for accepted, rejected (with reason), redirected, arrived, reviewed, no-show/unknown. A "sent" notification is NEVER equal to a referral completed.
**Step 5 — outcome closure:** Receiving team records outcome/decision within permitted scope; referral source is informed; patient's care timeline is updated. If no ACK or adverse condition, show escalation to actual clinician/reachable facility—not automated unsafe reassignment.
**Step 6 — maintain separation:** episode events are append-only with audited corrections; who/when/source/validity/verification; never silently cross-link two people; no AI authority promotion.
**Step 7 — safe failure:** offline-first is not plaintext storage. If protected local queue cannot guarantee encryption/role unlock/consent/deletion, do not persist sensitive data offline; use institution-approved paper and downtime procedure. Printed handoffs require disclosure controls; QR must never reveal PHI without authenticated server. Never assume geolocation or emergency number coverage.

## Concrete obligations and defensible innovation

- **Interoperability:** Compatible where justified with FHIR (e.g., Task, ServiceRequest, Composition/DocumentReference, MedicationStatement/MedicationUsage, Provenance, Encounter) once Nigeria Core FHIR profiles and permitted interfaces are independently verified. No invented national endpoints; adapters optional.
- **Closed-loop semantics:** distinct states draft, clinically reviewed, prepared, transmitted, received, accepted, arrived, completed, declined, redirected, expired/unreachable, superseded; state transitions authenticated, idempotent, traceable, and capable of explicit *unknown* status. Never auto-claim a receiving provider accepted a patient merely because an HTTP request succeeded.
- **Identity:** patient consent/authorization, identity assurance, clinical role verification, facility registry matching; if no national ID or reliable smartphone, the workflow cannot depend on one. Don't infer matching identities from names or age. Include safe manual exception resolution.
- **Nigerian language/literacy:** Hausa/Yoruba/Igbo/Pidgin among possible localizations, review translations with native-speaking clinicians; voice optional with explicit consent, multilingual transcription should display original text and uncertainty; no autonomous translations into diagnoses.
- **Power, bandwidth, and equipment:** low-end Android, intermittent data, outages, shared devices, unreliable printer, queues and understaffing; allow extremely short capture and pen-and-paper fallback. Interface never blocks urgent care for a login.
- **Governance:** patients should not be locked in, no exclusive national record ownership, data minimization, purpose limitation, role/time-bound authorization, encryption, event audit, breach and retention policy, NDPA/GAID compliance, DPIA and appropriate provider/ethics/regulatory approvals.
- **Clinical safety:** no herb efficacy, treatment, dosing or green interaction clearance. Product's clinical claims require validated guidelines, medical device classification review and human oversight. Existing Supabase unverified RLS and demo/provider bypass routes are critical blockers.
- **Economics and institutional gravity:** aim to become a **trusted, auditable open-standard component** serving multiple EHRs/PHCs, not a national data monopoly. Government interest, procurement or policy adoption are later outcomes only if objectively demonstrated; government-facing ambitions must not define initial revenue.

## The uncomfortable/impossible questions — answers must be evidence, not marketing

1. **Power outage:** What happens to every triage/referral when both sites lack electricity and connectivity for 48 hours? Is safe paper fallback faster? Can queued events avoid duplicate orders after reconnection?
2. **Wrong-person risk:** If patient has no valid national ID, several spellings and a shared family telephone, how does a clinician avoid merging the wrong patient?
3. **Receiver never responds:** Can sender distinguish transmitted, read, accepted, arrived and treated without lying? Who takes action in the next 15/60/240 minutes?
4. **No vacancy:** If the receiving facility has no bed/worker/drug, can the product avoid false promises or unsafe rerouting? Who confirms capacity?
5. **Unlicensed first contact:** If a patent medicine vendor, community volunteer or herbal practitioner is first, what may they safely record and when must a qualified worker take over?
6. **Critical symptom + bad network:** Could our interface delay emergency referral by forcing a questionnaire or subscription?
7. **Referral reversed:** A secondary clinic sends the patient back to PHC. Can the receiving note and change history be transported back without creating two truths?
8. **Triage wrong:** Can an independent safety assessor reproduce why the clinician assigned urgency, and is the patient harmed by software suggestions?
9. **Unidentified herb:** If a mixture contains unspecified ingredients and a misleading NAFDAC code, can we clearly say *identity unknown* without a fake safety verdict?
10. **Low clinician time:** Can the workflow be faster than a 1-page paper handoff rather than demanding 10 extra fields?
11. **Misaligned incentives:** Who *pays* to enter data when another hospital receives the benefit? Could one organization profit from deliberate underreporting?
12. **Data rights:** Can small clinics adopt without surrendering their patient records? Can a patient move to another vendor freely?
13. **Government HIE delayed:** Does the app still work for two facilities without any planned national API? Can it integrate later without a rewrite?
14. **Emergency dispatch misrepresented:** What if NEMSAS/112 is unavailable in a given locality? Do we ever imply an ambulance was actually dispatched or guaranteed?
15. **Clinical privacy under coercion:** If a phone is seized/shared or a provider abuses access, how can data exposure be detected and minimized?
16. **Corrupt credentials:** Can an attacker forge a clinician role, replay ACKs, or mark a referral completed from another tenant? Test all cross-site authorization paths.
17. **Power and finances:** Are cheap phones, free hosting tiers and donor credits reliable enough for healthcare, and what is the real lifecycle cost including supervision, security and support?
18. **Better baseline:** Would a printed, numbered referral slip, call-back and a shared WhatsApp group outperform MedPAi at the same task? If yes, should we build only a standardized protocol and low-cost adapter?
19. **Ability to pay:** Is the viable first buyer a private clinic group, employer clinic, PHC agency, state programme or payer? Do not infer from ministry budgets or World Bank loans.
20. **Value without lock-in:** Why would government favour a component whose interfaces are open and whose data they own? Can MedPAi create reliability, evaluation and integration advantages instead of captivity?

## Validation and commercial kill gates

**Gate 0 — governance:** Keep current Vercel preview separate from real patient data, unverified Supabase projects, unsafe demo provider bypass, unreviewed triage/safety content. Confirm NAFDAC SaMD, PCN, NDPC, professional and research consent path.
**Gate 1 — field reality (12–15 no-PHI interviews):** PHC/community first-contact professionals; receiving outpatient nurses/doctors; medical records officers; referral coordinators; pharmacy staff; state PHCDA/agency or private network procurement person. Ask for exact workflow, data losses, three last non-identifying examples, times, capacity and payer.
**Gate 2 — workflow baseline:** Compare 1-page paper/referral/phone vs existing EHR vs product on institution-approved anonymized or separately consented cases, adjudicated by licensed clinicians. Track source preservation, true discrepancies, mistaken links, "sent-not-received" false closure, confirmed receiver ACK, referral completion where independently auditable, staff minutes, patient consent comprehension, and adverse near-misses.
**Gate 3 — product engineering:** One sender organization and one receiver; clinician-authenticated transitions; signed event provenance and no-invented-data, minimal packet, one-way feedback closure; reliable downgrade to paper. Don't implement multi-state national network prematurely.
**Gate 4 — secure clinical pilot:** NDPA/privacy impact and institution/ethics authorizations, patient-rights process, security/RLS negative tests, independent clinical review, uptime/error reporting, no public demo clinic or fake user.
**Gate 5 — economy:** Document single workflow buyer, price and willingness-to-pay, per-handoff/time cost, professional supervision costs. If no adopter with a clear budget or paper workflow wins, pivot.

**Proposed pass thresholds are hypotheses to negotiate with clinicians; do not invent measured precision or government savings.** Any incorrect automatic urgency/identity link, harmful delay, uncontrolled cross-site data access, false referral completion or unbounded workflow burden is a kill issue.

## Design feedback discipline

Before every significant code change, write: source evidence / operator / clinical risk / simplified alternative / Nigerian real-world failure scenario / why it beats baseline / privacy and cost / test plan / owner / rollback / kill criterion. Keep PR draft and handoff current. A national milestone is an **outcome of real improvement**, not of calling a product “national”.

**Actual project status:** As of this note, the source app has a protected, session-only Vercel development preview; new medication evidence core and NLM terminology import proof; original triage/telepharmacy screens are not ready for clinical use; authentication and RLS are unverified; no field site, consented patient population, regulator acceptance, or government investment confirmed.


## Cross-project overlap and scope control (source-checked, 2026-10-10)

Existing sibling concept **Care Handoff** (read-only checked at `okoyeDelight/CAre-HAndoff-2` README and master handoff) already investigates attention-preserving **first-contact encounter capture** using a bounded, offline JS classifier, exact evidence spans, human review and an encrypted local handoff mechanism. Those are **engineering prototypes, not clinical validation**. The current MedPAi strategy would risk duplicating this work if it attempted to build another complete encounter-capture engine.

**Seam to preserve, not a repository merger:**
- Care Handoff expertise: a trained worker's within-encounter evidence capture and unresolved-question preservation, with offline-first limitations.
- MedPAi potential differentiated job: medication assertion lifecycle **across episodes** plus an authorization-backed **referral receipt/acceptance/outcome timeline** between sending and receiving care organisations.
- Potential future adapter: one human-approved handoff payload from encounter capture into an externally verifiable MedPAi care-transition object, preserving its original evidence state; no silent authority promotion and no real data transfer without permission.
- Do not automatically blend code, dependencies, synthetic data, model claims, user identities or backend patient information. Compare which approach actually saves frontline worker time before integrating. The founder may instead decide that Care Handoff or a standardized paper handoff is the better standalone solution, in which case MedPAi should pivot rather than clone.

**Open founder decision:** Is the first scarce resource to recover *clinician attention during encounter capture* or *reliable acknowledgment of referral and medicine continuity across two facilities*? These require different operators, pilots, value metrics, and buyers. Protect both hypotheses from premature feature expansion.
