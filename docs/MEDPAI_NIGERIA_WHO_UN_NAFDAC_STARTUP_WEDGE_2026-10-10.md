# MedPAi — Nigeria/WHO/UN/NAFDAC Startup Opportunity Research

**Date:** 2026-10-10. **Status:** Evidence and hypotheses for founder decision; not a validated business model or regulatory clearance.

## Executive conclusion
**Do not build:** another AI doctor/herbal-cure adviser, autonomous herb-drug interaction checker, electronic health record, medicine marketplace, stock platform, national drug registry or NAFDAC adverse-event reporting clone.
**Leading candidate to validate:** one professional-led **medication-reality reconciliation** workflow for an outpatient chronic-disease follow-up (hypertension or diabetes). Its differentiation must be explicit capture of traditional/herbal/OTC products alongside prescriptions, source provenance, discrepancies, longitudinal changes, uncertainty and deliberate professional verification. This is NOT a commitment to serve every patient/provider or an assertion that competitors lack the function.

## Local clinical evidence (do not generalize beyond sample)
- Nigerian 2023 two-tertiary-hospital nonrandomized study of medication reconciliation for ambulatory diabetes/hypertension patients reported poor baseline reconciliation and improvements after education. It does not prove software demand. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10652589/
- 2024 Anambra qualitative 25-informant study reports community-pharmacy adverse-event underreporting and barriers including awareness, motivation, feedback; not representative of all Nigeria. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-67263-8
- 2025 mixed Nigeria/Malawi study examined **28 herbal products collected in Ibadan** (from 2020–2021) and interviewed regulators: labeling, ingredient standardization and herbal pharmacovigilance problems. This is not a nationwide 2026 prevalence estimate. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12906-025-04812-2
- Older Lagos hypertensive clinic studies document concurrent traditional and conventional product use, but their rates must not be extrapolated to Nigeria today. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17903257/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20214258/

## Direct competitors / substitutes (vendor-reported capabilities, not audited)
- **MedSafe** offers Nigerian drug monographs, interaction checking, NAFDAC verification, PDF and formularies. Product claims/adoption numbers are vendor assertions, not independent audit: https://medsafe.com.ng/
- **Remedial Rx** advertises patient medication records and role-based access alongside pharmacy procurement, so a simple medication list is not defensible: https://www.remedialhealth.com/solutions/remedial-rx
- **DrugStoc** supply chain/stock; **Helium Health** provider EHR; **mPharma** pharmacies, medicines access and continuity. Do not duplicate their whole categories: https://drugstoc.com/ ; https://heliumhealth.com/ ; https://mpharma.com/
- NAFDAC Greenbook / Med Safety exist: they should be officially referenced rather than replaced.

## Competing wedge assessment
**A. Consumer herbal-dose/interaction chatbot — REJECT for MVP.** Weak reviewed efficacy/interaction data, potential patient harm, huge liability, crowded drug-reference market. No autonomous clearance.
**B. Herbal product documentation and ADR-report preparation for professionals — SECONDARY OPTION.** Strong NAFDAC/WHO alignment, but Med Safety already exists, regulatory integration/rights unknown, payer unproven. Test only if clinicians prioritize it.
**C. Mixed-source professional medication reconciliation — LEADING EXPERIMENT.** At ONE outpatient follow-up (e.g. hypertension or diabetes), compare prescription, actual-use account, other OTC/herbal products. Output source-backed candidate mismatches, unknowns and clinician/pharmacist-approved current-use snapshot. Do not claim a software tool improves safety until prospectively evaluated. Danger: existing PMRs may do enough, data-entry burden, inability to pay, lack of licensed reviewers.

## User, buyer, workflow, proof
- **Operator:** a licensed pharmacist/clinician conducting an outpatient follow-up at one type of care site (partner to decide). **Beneficiary:** patient and next authorized care provider. **Payer hypothesis:** licensed clinic/pharmacy operator responsible for quality, NOT WHO/UN/NAFDAC by default.
- **Core unit:** longitudinal medication assertion/episode: raw medicine/product name; prescribed vs dispensed vs reported use; source/evidence; date; started/changed/stopped/unknown; reviewer and verification state. Never promote an AI inference or reported use into authority.
- **Minimal deliverable:** structured intake of 2+ imperfect sources, explicit candidate discrepancies, uncertainty ledger, clinician/pharmacist review, signed/auditable handoff. Optional AI extraction only if it beats a deterministic/manual baseline. No diagnosis, prescribing or fabricated product properties.
- **Market test first:** 12–15 interviews across 2 comparable site types; observe existing forms and reconciliation burden without extracting patient data. Ask who owns a budget, what clinical encounter is costly and why existing tools are insufficient.
- **Benchmark:** simple normal medication-history form. Independently score source provenance, true discrepancies caught, false flags, review time, unknown preservation, errors and extra workload. Use expert-authored cases in *test harness only*, never prepopulated as real people in the product.
- **Proposed go/no-go criteria (hypotheses):** at least 2 licensed organizations volunteer as pilot design partners, 1 buyer verifies a concrete willingness-to-pay at a named price, meaningful improvement over a simple form without unsafe authority upgrades, and professionally acceptable time burden. If existing PMR already achieves the outcome, integrate with it or stop. If nobody pays or workflows worsen, stop.
- Real health studies/pilots require institution authorization, appropriate ethics/privacy review and secure backend/RLS first. Product current state is development, not patient-ready.

## Operational sequencing and constraints
1. **Stop expanding screens** before selecting one operator, moment and payer.
2. Complete 12–15 stakeholder interviews and competitor workflow audit, including MedSafe/Remedial and NAFDAC Med Safety.
3. Specify medication evidence assertions, conflict and human verification invariants; evidence and uncertainty first, AI optional.
4. Fix actual project identity/RLS/signup session authorization issues before real records; encryption, authorization, audit, consent, retention and deletion policies.
5. Validate on consented, legally approved real workflows with qualified professional oversight, and compare to a no-AI form.
6. Map to Nigeria NDHA's current official FHIR profiles once integration standards/rights are verified; do not invent a national HIE endpoint.
7. Build a repeatable paid product only after workflow and economic proof.

**Open questions:** purchaser and budget, exact outpatient setting, depth of incumbent reconciliation features, access/licence to national registries, full text of 2025–2029 Nigerian TM implementation plan, clinical software regulatory status, 2026 local discrepancy rates, and live backend. **No institution has approved, funded or partnered with MedPAi.**

**Method:** Evidence → what breaks → narrow surviving hypothesis → field/cost/safety constraints → kill criteria. This document records research direction, not product completion.
