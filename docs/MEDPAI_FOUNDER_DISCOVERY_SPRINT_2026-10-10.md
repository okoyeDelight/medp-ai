# MedPAi — First 14 Days of Founder Discovery

**Status:** Proposed field-study plan for founder approval. Not a recruiting claim; no organisations, participants, partners or purchases confirmed.

## Primary business hypothesis to falsify

A licensed outpatient hypertension-care professional encounters discrepant or unknown medicine histories, especially when people use prescribed products alongside OTC or traditional products. A provenance-aware, reviewable reconciliation workflow may deliver a measurable improvement over their existing form/process.

**Unit of value:** One reconciled medication-review encounter. **Human decision owner:** treating or consulting licensed healthcare professional. **Potential buyer:** pharmacy/clinic operator with purchasing authority, not automatically a UN/WHO/World Bank programme.

## Recruitment design

- 6–8 licensed community pharmacists or pharmacy leads and 6–8 outpatient doctors/nurses/pharmacists responsible for medication history at an outpatient setting; interviews compare **two** possible entry markets. Select ONE for first product; do not promise both.
- Recruitment must be consent-based. Explain we are exploring workflows and have no validated clinical tool. No recording without explicit permission.
- Do not collect patients' actual names, medicine histories, photos, diagnoses, records, visit dates or identifying stories. Ask participants to describe general workflows or walk through institution-approved anonymized procedures only.
- No clinical pilot, regulated workflow, PII collection, medicine-use data upload or study with patients until privacy, ethics/site approvals and backend safeguards are established.

## Questions to ask BEFORE presenting MedPAi

1. In which encounters must you establish a current medication history, and which roles do each step?
2. What do you do when the prescription, patient statement, pharmacy dispensing record and packaging disagree?
3. How do you record uncertain medicine identities, unfamiliar brands or herbal/traditional preparations? What do you leave blank?
4. Which information sources can you legitimately see at that moment? Which need separate permission?
5. Walk through the most recent *nonidentifying* example of a difficult medication-history task. What made it difficult? Do not reveal actual patient details.
6. How long does a routine review typically take versus a challenging one? What is the current process for checking that estimate?
7. Which existing EHR/PMR, paper form or digital system is used? Does it already track reports versus prescriptions, source provenance and clinician verification?
8. Who decides to adopt software? What is the approval, clinical oversight and procurement process? What would make a paid tool unjustified?
9. When internet or software fails, how is this task completed safely?
10. What specific demonstrated improvement would make a pilot worth allocating professional time or budget?

Only AFTER uncovering the workflow should the researcher show the proposed *nonclinical* information model and ask participants to challenge it. Avoid “Would you love an AI assistant?” or exaggerated safety promises.

## Structured evidence log

For each interview store **no PII**; assign opaque interview code and record only organisational role category, care setting type, current workflow summary, duration estimate (self-reported vs observed), shortcomings verified vs conjectured, existing software capabilities, cost/approval owner, expressed objections, new hypothesis, and permission to contact later. Do not publish identifiable interviews.

**Competitor test:** Assess whether Remedial Rx, existing local PMRs, hospital EHRs or a two-page paper form already support the same source-state separation. If so, consider interoperating instead of replacing.

## Pass/fail criteria (proposed and revisable)

**Problem evidence:** Repeated verified workflow pain at one chosen encounter; exact moment and operator established; not only anecdotal stories.
**Operator feasibility:** A licensed professional is willing to inspect a prototype under appropriate approvals without expecting autonomous medical conclusions or extra unpaid work.
**Buyer evidence:** A named *role* with budget authority identifies plausible paid pilot value; one documented priced willingness-to-pay experiment, not “sounds nice.”
**Safety evidence:** Compared with a good standard form, the product must not increase false discrepancies, information loss, review delays or mistaken verification. Clinical validation under qualified oversight required.
**Kill/pivot:** Existing software/form delivers equal value with less effort; no access to required data; clinically unsafe false authority; no buyer; unacceptable administrative burden; licensing/privacy blockers.

## Founder checkpoints

- **Day 3:** Choose two recruiting settings and contact strategy (with no assumption any participant agreed).
- **Day 7:** Review 5–7 anonymized workflow interviews and competitor coverage; amend hypotheses without rewriting product screens.
- **Day 14:** Decision meeting with evidence table: one operator, one payer, one use moment, baseline metrics, 3 largest adoption objections, go/pivot/kill.
- **Code only on evidence:** Current RxNorm reference import and medication-assertion tests form a foundation, not evidence that customers want this workflow.
